How mortgage payments work

The terms that shape your payment most.

Mortgage amount

The amount you borrow has the biggest impact on your payment size. A higher mortgage means higher payments and more total interest over time.

Amortization period

A longer amortization usually lowers each payment but often increases the total interest paid over the life of the mortgage.

Payment frequency

Monthly, bi-weekly, and weekly options change how often you pay and how cash flow feels throughout the year.

Fixed vs variable

Know the difference before you choose.

Fixed

Steadier payments.

A fixed mortgage keeps the rate consistent during the term, making budgeting simpler and payment expectations easier to manage.

Variable

More market-sensitive.

A variable mortgage may shift with interest rate changes, which can affect either your payment amount or how much goes toward interest.

Decision focus

Choose based on comfort.

The best fit depends on your risk tolerance, monthly flexibility, and how much certainty you want during the term.

Buyer budgeting guidance

Think beyond just the home price.

  • Get pre-approved before entering the market so you know your likely budget range.
  • Think about what monthly payment feels comfortable, not just what seems technically possible.
  • Leave room for property taxes, insurance, utilities, condo fees, and closing costs.
  • Use the mortgage calculator as a planning tool, then confirm the real numbers with your lender.
